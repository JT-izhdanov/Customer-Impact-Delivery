# Research 03 — Industry Evidence on Outcome-Based / Value-Based Services

**Question:** What does the evidence say about professional-services firms shifting to outcome-based selling and pricing — what works, what fails, what the transition requires?
**Method:** Web research, August 2026. Several primary pages were proxy-blocked; findings from search extracts flagged. Evidence grades noted per item.

---

## 1. Documented firm examples

### McKinsey (strongest documented large-firm example)
- CEO Bob Sternfels stated (HBR IdeaCast, Jan 2026) that **~25% of McKinsey's global fees now come from outcomes-based pricing**, expected to rise; the firm "underwrites" outcomes via joint business cases with clients. Driver explicitly cited: AI compressing delivery timelines makes hourly billing harder to justify. ([HBR podcast](https://hbr.org/podcast/2026/01/where-mckinsey-and-consulting-go-from-here); [HBR interview](https://hbr.org/2026/01/we-want-to-make-ourselves-better)) — CEO on record; percentages self-reported.

### Accenture
- Positions everything under "360° Value" — a *value-narrative and measurement* motion more than a pricing motion. A third-party claim of "18% of consulting revenue outcome-based (FY25)" could **not** be verified in SEC filings — treat as unverifiable.

### Outsourcing/BPO — where outcome pricing is most institutionalized
- Everest Group runs a research line on outcome pricing and recommends a **3-phase, 6–12 month transition starting with a pilot** ([Everest Group](https://www.everestgrp.com/outcome-based-pricing/)). An IDC-attributed stat (outcome-based outsourcing deals 22% → 38%, 2023–2025) is second-hand via a vendor blog — unverified.

### Mid-market / Microsoft channel
- **No published, quantified case of a mid-market Microsoft partner completing this transition was found — a genuine evidence gap** (and an opportunity: whoever documents it first owns the reference story).
- Avanade/Slalom use "outcome-led" marketing language; no documented commercial-model change found.

## 2. Pricing research: adoption, models, failure modes, preconditions

### Adoption reality
- T&M remains dominant in tech professional services, but the mix is moving. **SPI Research 2025 benchmark (500+ firms):** fixed-price margins 37.2% vs T&M 36.4%; **billable utilization 66.4% — lowest in survey history** — the effort-based model is being hollowed out from inside; GenAI used in 27.1% of projects. ([SPI 2025](https://forms.workday.com/content/dam/web/en-us/documents/reports/SPI_2025_Benchmark_Report.pdf?refCamp=7014X000001yvgK))
- Practitioner consensus (Forbes Tech Council, opinion-grade): most PS firms "aren't ready to price entirely on outcomes"; recommended **crawl-walk-run** — start hybrid (fixed fee + success payment) on projects with confident outcomes, strong relationships, clean baselines.

### Models (consistent taxonomy across sources)
1. Fixed fee + value bonus / success payment (most common entry point)
2. Gainshare / risk-share (% of measured gains, with deadbands, caps, floors)
3. Fee-at-risk (portion contingent on milestones/KPIs)
4. Value-based consumption (price per valuable unit) — TSIA's intermediate step
5. Full outcome pricing (rare; requires provider control over the outcome)

### Failure modes (converging across practitioner + academic sources)
- **Baseline/definition failure** — no clean baseline → gainshare creates more disputes than it resolves. The #1 documented failure mode.
- **Attribution disputes** — outcomes are co-produced with the client; multi-cause results.
- **Cash-flow strain** — costs upfront, variable fees later.
- **Exogenous volatility** — outcomes swing on factors outside provider control unless deadbands/caps are contracted.
- **Metric gaming** from poorly chosen KPIs.
- **Administrative overhead** — baselines, normalization, true-ups need real data plumbing.
- **Cultural failure** — comp and recognition still reward billed hours, silently reverting the firm.

### Academic evidence (peer-reviewed)
- Böhm, Backhaus, Eggert & Cummins (2016), survey of 259 buyers/sellers of outcome-based contracts: both sides attach higher benefits to *economic* outcome-based contracts; risks center on information asymmetry and moral hazard ([SagePub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2055563616669740)).
- OBC case research (9 contracts, 5 firms): performance varies substantially; **formal + informal coordination mechanisms and behavioral/information alignment** determine success.
- Consistent preconditions: (a) provider has meaningful **control over the outcome**; (b) **measurable, baselined metric agreed pre-contract**; (c) shared data access and jointly chosen methodology; (d) trust between senior sponsors; (e) hybrid structure with fixed base; (f) deadbands/caps/true-up rules in the contract.

## 3. Vendor value-engineering practices (the artifact stack to copy)

All four major vendors converge on the same artifact stack — **value hypothesis → discovery workshop → quantified business case (baseline, KPI tree, ROI/NPV) → executive value map → post-go-live value realization reviews:**

- **SAP Value Engineering** (~20 yrs): Outside-In assessments with peer benchmarks; Collaborative Value Assessments co-built with the customer; KPI/value-driver libraries.
- **Salesforce Business Value Services:** value discovery workshops → quantified business case → **value maps** linking org goals → capabilities → measurable outcomes.
- **ServiceNow "Now Value":** Envision → Create → Validate → Champion; milestones tied to measured value.
- **Microsoft:** the **Catalyst** program (Inspire → Design → Empower → Achieve) built envisioning + Business Value Assessment into partner pre-sales (~3-week deliverable); Microsoft also arms partners with **Forrester TEI** studies as business-case ammunition. Note: Catalyst as a brand has been de-emphasized since ~2023 — confirm current status.
- **GitLab publishes its entire value-consulting practice openly** ([handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/solutions-architects/sa-practices/business-value-consulting)) — a free public template.
- Vendor methodology *impact* claims are marketing-grade; the existence and structure of the practices is well documented.

## 4. Paid vs free discovery

- **No controlled study found** — practitioner consensus only: paid discovery increases client commitment, filters unqualified buyers, produces deeper problem definition. Rule of thumb: price discovery at **<10% of expected engagement budget**.
- Blair Enns / Win Without Pitching: "diagnose before we prescribe" — argued from positioning theory, no published metrics.
- The Microsoft channel has a proven third option: **vendor-funded assessments** ("free to the customer, paid by Microsoft") as pipeline generators. Run both tiers deliberately.

## 5. Analyst perspectives

### TSIA (most directly relevant)
- **LAER** (Land–Adopt–Expand–Renew): reframes engagement from project handoff to lifecycle revenue; adoption/expansion/renewal is where value is monetized ([LAER](https://www.tsia.com/blog/re-thinking-your-customer-engagement-model-with-laer)).
- **Outcome engineering** (Thomas Lah): "systematically mapping technology capabilities to specific business outcomes"; requires aligning product, services, and sales on a **tight set of target outcomes** ([primer](https://psprofessional.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Outcome-Engineering.pdf)).
- **State of PS 2026 / "PS 2.0":** PS 1.0 (billable effort, one-time projects) "is breaking" — AI cannibalizes effort-based models. Prescriptions: **services engineering** (standardize/package/price offers), PS-as-a-Service subscription revenue, written strategy embedding AI + renewable revenue. Diagnosis: "awareness is high, execution is lagging." ([State of PS 2026](https://www.tsia.com/blog/state-of-professional-services-2026-ai-economics))
- Pricing evolution ladder: effort-based → value-based consumption → outcome-based.

### SPI Research
- Utilization at all-time low (66.4%) while margins at 5-year highs — efficiency gains are going into fixed-price margin, not hours.

### Gartner (paywalled, worth pulling if we have a seat)
- "How to Lead With Business-Outcome-Based Engagements as an Emerging Service Provider" ([doc 3185227](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/3185227)) — directly on point.

## Transferable lessons for JourneyTeam

1. **Sell outcomes before you price outcomes.** Value-based *selling* (quantified business case, baselined KPIs, VRRs) is the prerequisite and delivers most of the benefit; outcome-based *pricing* is an optional later step.
2. **Crawl-walk-run hybrids, never pure outcome fees.** Fixed base + success kicker; pure outcome pricing fails on cash flow and attribution for firms without balance-sheet depth.
3. **Baseline or don't bother.** Contract the measurement methodology before work starts: jointly signed baseline, shared data access, deadbands/caps, true-up rules, change-notice clause.
4. **Only tie fees to outcomes we materially control.** Adoption, cycle time, deflection = contractible; client revenue growth = usually not.
5. **Copy the vendor value-engineering artifact stack — it's free R&D.** (GitLab's handbook is a public template.)
6. **Make the paid assessment the productized front door** (<10% of expected engagement), with a Microsoft-funded tier as the free front door.
7. **Institutionalize VRRs with dollars attached.** First review 60–90 days post-go-live; quarterly for top accounts; 3–4 metrics each tied to $ or %.
8. **Restructure around LAER, not project close** — adoption telemetry, customer-success-like function, renewable offers.
9. **Productize before promising outcomes** ("services engineering") — standardized offers with known delivery variance are the precondition for underwriting results.
10. **Fix comp and culture or the change dies** — utilization-based recognition silently reverts firms to hours.
11. **Plan for working-capital and rev-rec impact** of any success-payment component; involve finance/legal in deal design early.
12. **The mid-market playbook is undocumented — measure our own pilot** and treat the results as proprietary IP and marketing material.

# Research 04 — How Microsoft Partners Package Outcome-Led Offerings (2025–2026)

**Question:** How do comparable and leading Microsoft partners position and package outcome-led offerings, and what can we learn?
**Method:** Web research, August 2026. Many partner sites and Marketplace listings were proxy-blocked (403) — details from search summaries and PR flagged **[thin]** where applicable. Published pricing is rare.

## 2. Top-tier partners

- **Avanade — the reference model for Copilot value packaging:** Copilot Vision & Value Evaluation → M365 Copilot Assessment → **Copilot Value Accelerator (5 weeks:** readiness + gaps + high-value use cases + change enablement + **quantified time savings)** + a Copilot Value Framework; a whole "Copilot business transformation practice"; annual AI Value Report as demand-gen ("up to 4× return per $1" — partner-commissioned). Client stories branded outcome-first ("What matters to X…"). No public pricing.
- **Neudesic (IBM):** assessment IP branding (Digital WorkforceIQ™), agentic/digital-workforce narrative. **[thin on mechanics]**
- **Insight:** M365 Copilot Deployment & Readiness Services; 1-day Copilot Envisioning Workshop. Pricing "varies by scope."
- **PwC / EY:** lead with **their own deployment as the proof asset** (PwC 285K Copilot users, "1M+ hours freed"; EY 150K users, "15% productivity boost" + a tailored **benefits framework** for value realization). EY–Microsoft announced a **$1B+/5-year joint initiative** on scaling enterprise AI value (May 2026). Top tier sells "value realization" as a discipline monetized via large programs, not priced entry SKUs.

## 3. Canonical packaged-assessment pattern

- **Names:** "[Product] Readiness Assessment" / "Envisioning Workshop" / "Quick Start" / "Accelerator" / "Proof of Value."
- **Durations:** 1 hr (free teaser) → 1 day (envisioning) → 2–4 wks (assessment) → 5–8 wks (pilot/value accelerator).
- **Pricing:** mostly hidden. Public data points: Noventiq 2-wk Copilot Readiness from $5K; Western Computer $15K/$30K/$45K tiers; many free 60–90-min entries. Microsoft ECIF/CSI funding underwrites much of the "free assessment" economy.
- **Deliverables:** readiness scorecard, prioritized use cases, governance gaps, ACM plan, roadmap, and increasingly a **business case** (Microsoft provides partners a "Business Case Builder" tool).
- **Laddering mechanics:** (a) assessment → pilot → deployment → managed service; (b) **fee-credit into implementation** (Western Computer 50%); (c) Microsoft-funded assessment as free front end; (d) "Proof of Value" engagements with ROI-realization + Copilot Dashboard implementation. **Post-deployment measurement is emerging as its own billable stage.**

## 4. Which framing dominates

- **Tech-led framing still dominates the mid-market** (nav organized by product) — including JourneyTeam today.
- **Industry-led is the differentiation layer for the winners** (Sikich, HSO, Hitachi, Quisitive verticals).
- **Pure business-outcome framing is rare and mostly rhetorical** (HSO's brand, Avanade's "What matters"). Almost nobody prices or contracts on outcomes publicly. Outcome language concentrates in AI offers because **Copilot ROI anxiety is the current buying trigger**.
- Net winning pattern: **industry-led packaging + outcome-flavored AI entry offers + tech-led delivery depth underneath.** No comparable mid-market partner publicly does true outcome-based commercial terms.

## 5. Published customer-impact metrics as differentiator

- Sparse everywhere. Best examples are product-page metrics (Hitachi), self-referential deployment stats (PwC/EY), and partner-commissioned research (Avanade). Mid-market case studies remain qualitative ("streamlined," "improved visibility").
- **A partner that systematically publishes client-verified business metrics would be unusual.** PwC/EY route around client confidentiality by instrumenting themselves.

## Positioning gaps and opportunities for JourneyTeam

1. **Published fixed-price assessment tiers are nearly unclaimed territory** (only Western Computer does it). A "Customer Impact Assessment" with public tiered pricing + implementation credit-back would be immediately differentiated.
2. **"Value realized" proof is the biggest whitespace.** Publishing 10–20 quantified, client-verified impact metrics (baseline → measured result) would out-position everyone in our weight class; the measurement mechanism exists (Copilot Dashboard, ERP KPIs, Secure Score).
3. **Measurement-as-a-service is emerging but unowned in mid-market.** A packaged post-go-live quarterly "Impact Review" subscription differentiates, creates recurring revenue, and manufactures case-study raw material.
4. **Copilot readiness assessments are commoditized** (dozens of near-identical 2-week offers from $5K). Differentiate at the *business case + measured pilot* level (Avanade's Value Accelerator pattern), not another readiness checklist.
5. **Ladder design beats offer design:** free educational front door → paid assessment → implementation with credit-back → managed impact. Articulate the whole ladder publicly.
6. **Industry editions are the credibility multiplier for outcome claims** — outcome metrics are only believable per-industry. Pick 2–3 verticals per play and attach impact benchmarks.
7. **Exploit Microsoft's adoption-incented FY26/FY27 economics:** Microsoft-funded impact assessments ("free to customer, paid by Microsoft") as top of funnel; requires keeping specializations current.
8. **A distinct outcome-vocabulary brand family** (e.g., Impact Briefing / Impact Assessment / Impact Sprint / Impact Review) stands out against "Accelerator/Quick Start" sameness and matches the Customer Impact strategy name.
9. **Nobody in the set publicly contracts on outcomes** — opportunity and caution: no proven mid-market template; pilot credit-back or milestone-linked terms before promising gainshare.

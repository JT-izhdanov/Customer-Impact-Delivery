# Research 02 — Why Reorgs Don't Produce Cross-Selling (and What Does)

**Question:** What does organizational research and practitioner evidence say about why structural mergers fail to produce cross-practice collaboration in professional services, and what actually drives it?
**Method:** Web research, August 2026. Several primary sources (HBR, McKinsey, Harvard CLP) were only reachable via search extractions — exact figures flagged. Sources named per item.

---

## 1. Heidi Gardner — the canonical research on cross-practice collaboration

Dr. Heidi K. Gardner (Harvard Law School, ex-McKinsey). Books: *Smart Collaboration* (HBR Press 2017), *Smarter Collaboration* (2022). Foundational article: ["When Senior Managers Won't Collaborate," HBR March 2015](https://hbr.org/2015/03/when-senior-managers-wont-collaborate). Dataset: a decade-plus of billing/HR/financial records across dozens of professional services firms — research, not vendor marketing.

**The economics (core empirical finding):**
- Revenue per client grows **non-linearly with the number of practice groups serving that client** — reported figures from her law-firm data: ~$200K average annual revenue per client at 4 practice groups → ~$600K at 6 → ~$900K at 7 (via search extraction; treat exact dollars as approximate).
- A client relationship spanning **5 practice groups generates ~18× the revenue of a single-practice engagement** ([Harvard CLP, "Collaboration in Law Firms"](https://clp.law.harvard.edu/article/collaboration-in-law-firms/)).
- **Stickiness:** multi-practice clients stay *even when the lead relationship partner leaves* — they become "owned by the firm, not by a particular partner." Directly relevant: single-practice clients are one departing seller away from churn.
- Collaboration also correlates with higher margins (complex multi-practice work is less price-shopped) and better talent retention.

**Why collaboration doesn't happen (her diagnosis):**
- **Asymmetric payoffs:** firm benefits are collective and long-term; individual costs (lost control of "my" client, shared credit, delivery risk by others) are immediate. Systems reward "rock stars, not the whole band."
- **Two kinds of trust are prerequisites:** *competence trust* (your practice will deliver quality for my client) and *interpersonal trust* (you won't steal the relationship). Both must exist.
- **Directly on point for JourneyTeam:** firms formed through **mergers specifically lack competence trust — "people just don't know how good the other players are."** A practice merger changes reporting lines but creates neither kind of trust. ([performanceleader.com interview with Gardner](https://www.performanceleader.com/publications/dr-heidi-k-gardner-on-smarter-collaboration-performance-reward))
- **Cross-selling ≠ collaboration.** Gardner: cross-selling is "Do you want fries with that?" — "clients despise cross-selling and feel it's being done *to* them instead of *for* them." Smart collaboration starts from the client's strategic problem and assembles the firm behind it. ([gardnerandco.co](https://www.gardnerandco.co/insights/smart-collaboration/))
- Notable claim: collaboration can be fostered even where comp can't be changed — comp is *a* lever, not *the* lever (her assertion, not independently verified).

## 2. Restructuring alone does not change behavior

- **McKinsey Global Survey, ["The secrets of successful organizational redesigns"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/the-secrets-of-successful-organizational-redesigns-mckinsey-global-survey-results):** only **~23% of reorgs are deemed successful**. Leading failure cause: treating redesign as a **lines-and-boxes exercise** while ignoring processes, performance management, and people. Success ~4× likelier with a leader-owned change narrative.
- **Heidari-Robinson & Heywood, ["Getting Reorgs Right," HBR Nov 2016](https://hbr.org/2016/11/getting-reorgs-right):** **>80% of reorgs fail to deliver intended value in the planned time; ~10% actively damage the company.** Explicit failure mode: "organizational chart changes without behavioral change."
- **Galbraith Star Model** ([jaygalbraith.com](https://jaygalbraith.com/services/star-model/)): performance = alignment of five design points — Strategy, Structure, **Processes, Rewards, People**. Structure only allocates decision power; lateral processes and rewards make behavior stick. Change the boxes without the other four and you recreate old problems in bigger boxes. Value is created "in the white spaces between units" ([Galbraith, "Organizing to Deliver Solutions," USC CEO 2002](https://ceo.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2002/07/2002_14-g02_14-Organizing_to_Deliver_Solutions.pdf)).
- **Maister, "The One-Firm Firm"** (SMR 1985; [davidmaister.com](https://davidmaister.com/articles/1/101/)): collaborative firms succeed via deliberate practices — firmwide (not practice-level) profit centers, group-accomplishment comp, heavy internal familiarity investment. **Practice-level P&Ls are explicitly identified as collaboration-killers.**
- **McKinsey, ["Capturing the value of 'one firm'"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/capturing-the-value-of-one-firm):** behavior shifted when sales managers *opened client books* to other units and early joint wins made gains tangible.

## 3. Incentive design for cross-selling: what works, what backfires

**Crediting mechanics (practitioner consensus — Alexander Group, WorldatWork):**
- 42% of companies credit one seller only; 58% credit two or more ([Alexander Group](https://www.alexandergroup.com/insights/seven-vexing-sales-compensation-issues/)).
- **Double crediting** (full credit to both relationship owner and service-line seller) is the standard answer for partner-model firms — but audit it: investigate when compensable revenue exceeds ~112% of actual; use system-validated crediting ([WorldatWork](https://worldatwork.org/publications/workspan-daily/double-sales-crediting-when-and-why-to-apply-it)).
- Rule-of-thumb blend for professional services: ~60–70% individual + 30–40% team/practice-level in variable comp (vendor content; treat as heuristic).
- Pick **one crediting event in time** (booking or invoice) — split-timing crediting breeds disputes.

**What backfires (evidence):**
- **Pure origination credit** → hoarding; the single most-cited cross-selling killer in law firms.
- **Fully pooled credit** → free riders.
- **Volume-based cross-sell targets** → Wells Fargo: "eight is great" cross-sell counts produced ~3.5M fake accounts, ~$3B in settlements ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo_cross-selling_scandal)). Never quota raw cross-sell counts.
- **Indiscriminate cross-selling destroys profit** — Shah & Kumar, ["The Dark Side of Cross-Selling," HBR Dec 2012](https://hbr.org/2012/12/the-dark-side-of-cross-selling) + *Journal of Marketing* 2012: across five firms, **10–35% of cross-buying customers are unprofitable and account for 39–88% of total customer losses.** Cross-sell selectively into good accounts.
- **Behavior + pipeline metrics beat revenue-only metrics in year one:** score internal referrals, specialist introductions, joint pursuits, qualified cross-practice opportunities — because individual payoffs from collaboration lag by design ([Association for Accounting Marketing](https://accountingmarketing.org/hot-topic-your-guide-to-creating-and-measuring-a-successful-cross-selling-incentive-program/)).

## 4. Account management models that drive multi-line penetration

- **RAIN Group benchmark (n=397 orgs with formal strategic account management):** top performers are 3.1× more likely to grow strategic-account revenue 20%+; **53% of top performers have an effective account growth process vs 21% of the rest** — a defined process is the differentiator, not talent ([RAIN](https://rainsalestraining.com/blog/new-research-benchmark-report-on-top-performance-in-strategic-account-management)).
- **Law-firm client teams** (closest analog): the primary working cross-sell mechanism. BD cost on existing relationships ≈ 10–15% of revenue vs 30–40% for new clients ([Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/legal/marketing-partner-forum-client-teams/)). Caveat: programs stall without a named leader, goals, and inspected measures.
- **QBR/value-review cadence + penetration mapping** (service lines × client business units, whitespace visible) is the consensus working artifact (vendor sources; mechanism is consensus, ROI claims are marketing).
- Synthesis with Gardner: account teams work because they manufacture *competence trust* — repeated joint exposure of specialists in front of a shared client.

## 5. Failure modes of "solutions/outcomes" overlays on practice structures

- **Galbraith (USC 2002; *Designing the Customer-Centric Organization*):** companies routinely fail at "solutions" by bolting a customer-facing overlay onto product/practice-centric units **without giving it real power**. Required: a front-end unit with its own metrics, plus five lateral processes (customer strategy, portfolio planning, solutions development, solutions pricing, team assembly/disassembly). A "solutions" brand with no pricing authority, no staffing authority, and no P&L-relevant metrics is the classic failure.
- **Davis & Lawrence, ["Problems of Matrix Organizations," HBR 1978](https://hbr.org/1978/05/problems-of-matrix-organizations):** matrix pathologies — power struggles, decision strangulation (everything escalates), layering driven by politics.
- Services-firm echo: utilization vs account-growth tug-of-war when practice leaders own the bench and account leaders own the client but **neither owns the outcome**.

## Design principles for JourneyTeam (grounded in the research)

1. **Name the failure correctly:** the merger changed structure — one of Galbraith's five design points — and left processes, rewards, people systems, and the client-facing motion unchanged. Old behavior reproduced itself in bigger boxes. This matches the base rate (~75–80% of reorgs fail this way).
2. **Make the economics the headline, not "collaboration" as a value:** revenue per client grows non-linearly with practices involved (~18× at 5 practices), and multi-practice clients survive personnel departures.
3. **Adopt "practices per client" as a top strategic KPI** (% of top accounts using 2+/3+ practices), inspected quarterly. Hard to game, directly tied to Gardner's curve.
4. **Fix the trust gap the merger created:** merged units lack *competence trust*. Engineer forced familiarity — cross-practice deal reviews, internal "what my practice actually does + proof" roadshows, co-staffing specialists on flagship projects. Trust is built through joint work, not announcements.
5. **Build the engine around named client teams for top accounts:** account lead + specialists from ≥2 practices, written account plan with whitespace/penetration map, QBR/value-review cadence. Process maturity is the differentiator (53% vs 21%); existing-client BD costs a third of new-logo BD.
6. **Sell client problems, not other practices:** clients hate being cross-sold. Anchor pursuits in multi-practice outcomes; assemble practices behind the client's problem.
7. **Fix crediting before asking for behavior:** double-credit cross-practice deals, audit at ~112% total credited revenue, one crediting event, system-validated. Never quota cross-sell counts (Wells Fargo).
8. **Reward leading behaviors in year one** (introductions, joint pursuits, cross-practice proposals) because individual payoffs lag by design.
9. **If an "outcomes" overlay is built, give it real power or don't build it:** convening authority, staffing pull, and leadership-inspected metrics at minimum (Galbraith; Davis & Lawrence).
10. **Cross-sell selectively and prove it early:** pick flagship accounts with real expansion economics (Shah & Kumar), manufacture visible joint wins in H1, and have leaders tell the story constantly — tangible early wins + leader-owned narrative is what flips culture (McKinsey).

# 07 — Operating Model & Incentives

**This is the document that decides whether Customer Impact works.** The practice merger failed because incentives, attribution, and staffing mechanics never changed. If this doc doesn't get adopted, the rest of the plan is a slide deck.

## Design principle

> Make the collaborative deal the *lazy* path. Sellers and practice leads should make more money, with less friction, on multi-practice outcome deals than on siloed ones.

## 1. Attribution rules — written, standard, non-negotiable per deal

The per-deal argument about splits is the single biggest tax on collaboration. Remove it by deciding once:

- **Revenue attribution:** delivery revenue books to the practice that delivers each workstream (follow the hours). No debate.
- **Sales credit:** the selling rep gets **100% quota credit for the full deal value regardless of practice mix** — a seller should never shrink a deal to keep it inside their comfort stack.
- **Origination credit:** the practice that sourced the opportunity gets a fixed origination percentage recognized in its P&L scorecard (proposed: 10% shadow credit, cost-free — a scorecard metric, not a cash transfer).
- **Cross-sell from VRRs:** opportunity surfaced in a Value Realization Review credits the Outcome Owner's practice as originator. This pays practices for tending accounts, not just landing them.

Whatever the final numbers, the rule is: **published before the fiscal year, applied automatically, revisited annually — never negotiated inside a live deal.**

## 2. Compensation changes (FY27)

- **Sellers:** add a multi-practice accelerator (proposed: +10–20% commission rate on deals with 2+ practice workstreams above threshold). Fund it from the higher deal sizes the motion produces.
- **Practice directors:** add a shared metric to each PD's bonus — same number for all four (proposed: % of company bookings that are multi-practice). Shared metrics end practice-vs-practice zero-sum behavior at the top.
- **Delivery leads / Outcome Owners:** recognition + bonus linkage to documented VRR impact records, not just utilization.

## 3. Deal desk (lightweight)

A function, not a committee — one rotating architect-level person per week plus the relevant PDs on call:

- Reviews every deal above threshold for the second-practice opportunity (the 30-minute review in doc 05).
- Owns the Impact Hypothesis quality bar.
- Arbitrates the rare attribution edge case *by the published rules*, not by negotiation.

## 4. Cross-practice capacity view

- A single forward view of bench capacity across all four practices (skills + availability), visible to all PDs and the deal desk.
- Without it, "we can't staff the second practice's workstream" quietly kills the model. With it, pods staff in days.
- Tooling: whatever we already use for resourcing, extended — this is a process requirement, not a software purchase.

## 5. Governance cadence

| Cadence | Forum | Looks at |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Deal desk | Deals above threshold, hypothesis quality |
| Monthly | PD council (4 PDs + sales leader) | Leading metrics (doc 08), play performance, staffing friction |
| Quarterly | Leadership | Lagging metrics, comp/attribution tuning, case studies, kill/scale decisions per play |

## 6. What we deliberately do NOT do

- **No re-org.** Four practices stay. Customer Impact is a motion they run together, not a fifth box.
- **No shared-P&L merger.** Practices keep their P&Ls; attribution rules handle the seams. Merging P&Ls would repeat the last mistake — structural change without behavioral mechanics.
- **No mandatory collaboration quotas on individual deals.** The threshold review + accelerators pull; we don't push single-practice deals into artificial partnerships.

## Notes

- All percentages are proposals to seed the comp-design conversation in Q4 2026; finance and sales leadership own final numbers.
- Legal/finance should sanity-check that shadow origination credit doesn't create revenue-recognition confusion — it's a scorecard construct only.

## Purpose

Launch activates the solution by deploying to production, enabling users, and ensuring a smooth transition to operations. It begins once delivery is complete and continues through stabilization until the solution is fully adopted and ready for business as usual.

## Exit gate — Project Wrap

Before moving from Launch to Evolve:
1. **Customer Signoff**
   1. Is the Flag Plant on track to be realized, or have outcomes been realized?
   2. Is the project ready to transition to support?
2. **Operate**
   1. The project team disengages from daily operations
   2. Transition to business as usual, with the client operation team taking responsibility for day-to-day activities
3. **Project Retro & Sales Handoff**
   1. Customer Retro: PM sends email to begin retro process
      - Projects ≥ 100 hours and Sherpa → handled by Customer Advocacy
      - Projects < 100 hours and not Sherpa → handled by Sales (SAM or AE)
   2. Sales Handoff: agreement to close project
4. **Project Close**

## Customer Impact integration points

Launch is where the plan's biggest structural finding lives — the exit gate itself asks the impact question and then disengages before it can be answered:

- **Exit criterion 1.1 already asks the right question:** *"Is the Flag Plant on track to be realized, or have outcomes been realized?"* The honest answer at go-live is almost always **"on track, not yet realized"** — business metrics move weeks-to-months after deployment. Today that question gets a judgment-call answer and the team disengages (criterion 2). The plan's answer: the signoff records the *measured baseline + adoption telemetry + the scheduled first VRR* — converting "on track" from an opinion into a dated, instrumented commitment that survives disengagement.
- **"Referenceable Customer" and the VRR are the same motion, sequenced.** A customer asked for a reference at project wrap can only reference the *experience*. Asked after a VRR documents metric movement, they can reference the *result* — the quantified case-study evidence research 04 identified as the mid-market's biggest whitespace. Proposed: for impact engagements, the reference ask follows the first VRR, not project close; Customer Advocacy (who already owns ≥100hr retros) owns both.
- **The Stabilize sub-stage is adoption's proving ground.** Stabilization already watches the environment; for impact engagements it also watches the adoption telemetry configured in Deliver — usage below threshold at stabilization end is a flag to Evolve/support, not a closed project's silent failure. This is also where Microsoft's MAU-based incentives are won or lost (research 01).
- **Sales Handoff meets the account plan.** The handoff that closes the project should hand the *outcome thread* to whoever owns the account relationship: hypothesis status, first VRR date, and the "next-highest-impact opportunity" slot the VRR will fill (doc 06). This is the LAER Land→Adopt seam (research 03) — the exact point where the diagnosis said "engagements end at go-live" (cause 7).
- **The Evolve stage exists — that's the structural good news.** The Proven Process doesn't actually end at launch; it has a fifth stage. The plan's post-go-live machinery (VRRs, impact records, managed impact) should live *inside Evolve* rather than beside it — mapping to follow when Evolve content arrives.

### Watch (routed to backlog)

Criterion 2 (team disengages from daily operations) and the plan's post-go-live measurement cadence must not be read as contradictory: the *project team* disengages; the *Outcome Owner* and the VRR cadence persist into Evolve. If "disengage" is applied to the Outcome Owner too, the model breaks at exactly the point the whole plan exists to fix. Needs an explicit line in the RASIC when it lands.

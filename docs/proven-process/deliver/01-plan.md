# Deliver 1 of 3 — Plan (Internal Planning)

*Source: internal Proven Process content provided by Igor Zhdanov, Aug 2026.*

Successful internal planning brings JourneyTeam together to prepare for execution by defining sprint capacity, refining the backlog, and aligning on clear commitments. Teams ensure work is prioritized, understood, and ready to move forward.

## Key actions

- Define Sprint Capacity, Groom Backlog & lock in Commitments
- Commitments can range from designing, architecting, configuring & building systems, workflows, dashboards, integrations, migrations
- Flag vague items; discuss and document changes

## What "great" looks like

- 3 Sprints planned and ready
- Backlog is prioritized
- Commitments are clearly defined
- Risks and dependencies are documented
- Team is aligned on next steps

## Customer Impact integration points

Sprint planning is where impact intent survives — or dies — at the task level:

- **Backlog grooming uses the metric lens.** Stories arrived from Plan-stage Discovery tagged to outcome metrics; grooming prioritizes metric-moving stories when capacity forces trade-offs (and it will). The tag does the arguing — no new ceremony.
- **"Flag vague items" includes vague impact claims.** A story tagged to a metric with no plausible mechanism ("dashboard → forecast accuracy, somehow") is a vague item by the sub-stage's own standard — flag it, sharpen it or strip the tag. This keeps metric traceability honest instead of decorative.
- **Measurement tasks are commitments, not chores.** Baseline refreshes, telemetry configuration, adoption instrumentation get planned as sprint commitments alongside build work (they're already EOW deliverables, per `envision/06`). What isn't in a sprint doesn't happen.
- **Cross-practice dependencies get named per sprint.** For pod engagements (doc 06), each sprint plan states which practice's work depends on which — the operational level where "cross-practice delivery" is real or isn't, and where the capacity view (doc 07) earns its keep.

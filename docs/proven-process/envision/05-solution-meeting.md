# Envision 5 of 6 — Solution Meeting

*Source: internal Proven Process content provided by Igor Zhdanov, Aug 2026.*

During the Solution Meeting, JourneyTeam presents the recommended solution and demonstrates how it will achieve the customer's desired outcomes. This stage confirms alignment on the approach, value, and expectations before moving toward a formal engagement.

## Key actions

- Present how JourneyTeam will achieve desired outcomes with measurable results.
- Provide any showcase if needed.
- Agree on [A-Grade Flag Plant](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/ProjectDelivery/SitePages/Project-Flag-Plant.aspx) and share pricing and ROI.
- Ensure both Prospect and JourneyTeam agree on:
  - Business Outcomes
  - Issues jeopardizing success
  - [A-Grade Flag Plant](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/ProjectDelivery/SitePages/Project-Flag-Plant.aspx)
  - Prospect acknowledgment of expectations met.
- Leverage our Envision Sales Agent to provide Coaching and suggested next steps.

## What "good" looks like

- Solution is clearly presented and understood
- Approach aligns to goals and [Flag Plant](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/ProjectDelivery/SitePages/Project-Flag-Plant.aspx)
- Scope and timeline are clear
- Questions and risks are addressed
- Stakeholders are aligned and ready to move forward

## Customer Impact integration points

The Solution Meeting's required mutual agreements are already 80% of the Impact Hypothesis sign-off — the plan closes the remaining 20%:

- **"Business Outcomes" agreement gains numbers.** Today the agreement can be satisfied qualitatively; for above-threshold deals the plan requires the outcome stated as *metric + baseline + target + date* (the signed Impact Hypothesis, doc 02). The agreement item already exists in the Proven Process — only its precision changes.
- **"Share pricing and ROI" is the business-case moment.** The ROI presented here should *be* the Impact Hypothesis value estimate (customer's numbers, our math shown) — one artifact, not a sales ROI slide and a separate delivery baseline that never reconcile. Where the account is funding-eligible or Microsoft-managed (a subset in our SMB/SMC base), the consumption/adoption forecast for funding claims attaches here too (research 01).
- **"Issues jeopardizing success" doubles as the customer's 50%.** Block's contracting requirement (session 01): the issues list must include what the *customer* owns — data access, exec sponsorship, adoption effort — and what happens when it isn't delivered. Agreeing on it here makes the EOW's mutual obligations uncontroversial.
- **"Prospect acknowledgment of expectations met" is the anti-self-orientation gate.** If the honest recommendation is *not* to buy (or to buy less), saying so here is what builds the trusted-advisor position the whole model depends on (Maister, session 01).
- **Measurement plan lands here, not after signing:** who measures, what data, first VRR date — presented as part of the solution so it flows into the EOW (sub-stage 6) without renegotiation.

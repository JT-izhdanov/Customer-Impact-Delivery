# Envision 6 of 6 — EOW Sent

*Source: internal Proven Process content provided by Igor Zhdanov, Aug 2026.*

The Envision phase concludes with the delivery of an Estimate of Work (EOW). This document outlines the agreed-upon solution, scope, and costs, providing the foundation for the customer to move forward with the engagement.

## Key items needed in an EOW

- A-Grade Flag Plant included in EOW
- Provide details around how we are going to deliver the Solution
- Include details and Costs

## What "good" looks like

- EOW reflects agreed scope, timeline, and outcomes
- Pricing and deliverables are clear
- Stakeholders are aligned and ready to approve
- Questions are minimal and addressed quickly
- Next steps to signature are clear

## Customer Impact integration points

The EOW is where the outcome commitment becomes contractual — three additions for above-threshold deals, none of which change the EOW's structure:

- **Impact Hypothesis rides with the Flag Plant.** The EOW already requires the A-Grade Flag Plant; above threshold, the signed Impact Hypothesis attaches alongside it (proposed fourth exit-gate item, see stage doc). "EOW reflects agreed... outcomes" then means measurably, not rhetorically.
- **The measurement plan is a deliverable line.** Baseline capture, adoption telemetry setup, and the first Value Realization Review (60–90 days post-go-live, doc 06) appear in the EOW's delivery details and costs — so post-go-live measurement is bought, not begged for later.
- **Modularize for funding claimability.** Structure multi-practice EOWs so each workload-aligned module is separately identifiable — this is what lets Sales Ops stack ECIF / MCI / Azure Accelerate funding and claim per-workload PoE without rework (research 01; Go-To-Market sales-ops support).
- **Pricing maturity guardrail:** 2027 EOWs stay fixed/phased fee with impact *measured and reported* — no fees contingent on business outcomes yet (doc 05 pricing ladder). The EOW language should promise measurement, never underwrite results we don't control.

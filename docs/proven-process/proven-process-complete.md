# The JourneyTeam Proven Process

*Complete reference — compiled from internal Proven Process content provided August 2026. This document contains the process as documented; Customer Impact integration commentary lives in the per-stage docs in this folder. Internal links point to JourneyTeam SharePoint/Dynamics/Power BI and are access-controlled.*

The Proven Process has **five stages**, run as a **cycle**: Evolve's exit gate (a new non-support project lead) feeds Envision.

| # | Stage | Theme | Led by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Envision** | Leading the Process | Marketing, Sales, Presales |
| 2 | **Plan** | Building the Roadmap | Project Management, Delivery |
| 3 | **Deliver** | Bringing the Solution to Life | Delivery (CXC), COO |
| 4 | **Launch** | Going Live with Confidence | PMO, COO |
| 5 | **Evolve** | Becoming a Trusted Advisor | Customer Advocacy, Support, Account teams |

**Project roles (every project, all stages):** Project Manager (delivery governance) · Project Lead (technical direction) · Project Developers (pool of technical resources). Governed by the PM/PL Partnership operating standard (v1.0, July 2026).

# Stage 1: Envision — Leading the Process

The Envision stage lays the foundation for success by aligning strategy, value, and partnership before delivery begins. It starts with the first interaction with a potential customer and continues until a new project is confirmed through a signed EOW. Marketing, Sales, and Presales are the primary roles responsible for driving this stage.

**Who leads:** Marcel Narish (CMO) · Drew Peterson (CRO) · Mark Mickelsen (VP of Client Solutions) · Jordan Douglas (Sales Director). Full RASIC chart coming soon.

**Method:** the JourneyTeam Envision Sales Process, which focuses on driving Business Outcomes by using Status and Scenario questions.

## How success is measured

**Booked Revenue** — the total value of signed EOWs representing sales that have been closed and committed by the customer. Tracked in [Dynamics](https://jtp.crm.dynamics.com/main.aspx?appid=3c081701-b27e-e911-a81d-000d3a3b5933&pagetype=entitylist&etn=opportunity&viewid=aaaaec95-7286-e911-a819-000d3a3b53c4&viewType=1039&extraqs=visualizationid%3D2b462c77-0daf-e911-a82f-000d3a3b54ca%26visualizationPaneMode%3D1) against 2026 PS Booked Revenue targets.

Leading indicators:
- **Lead Creation:** Sales & Marketing generate 338 Sales Qualified Leads (SQLs) per month — qualified interest from potential customers through marketing, partner programs, and sales outreach.
- **New Opportunities (Pipeline Created):** $9M in new pipeline (183 new opportunities a month) — a qualified lead with a clear business need advanced for discovery, solution alignment, and pursuit.
- **Close Percentage:** close at least 57% of opportunities (signed EOW).

## Sub-stage 1.1 — Go-To-Market

Drives pipeline and revenue by positioning JourneyTeam's value in the market, building awareness, and creating opportunities to engage the right customers.

- **Define Targeting & Messaging** — partner with Sales and Practice Leads to align on target accounts, messaging frameworks, and offer positioning.
- **Execute Demand Generation** — design and run multi-channel campaigns that nurture prospects from initial awareness through qualification and handoff to Sales.
- **Optimize Performance** — use data and insights to continuously refine targeting, messaging, and channel effectiveness.
- **Measure & Improve** — track key metrics (open rates, reply rates, conversions, influenced pipeline) and provide clear, actionable recommendations.
- **Enable Sales Execution** — develop and maintain content and assets that support both brand awareness and sales engagement.
- **Activate Customer Advocacy** — identify and engage satisfied customers to build references, case studies, and success stories.
- **Sales Operations Support** — enables efficient lead flow and revenue execution: support Sales participation in Microsoft GTM programs (Partner Center lead sharing, funded engagement processes); ensure CPOR is properly claimed; support CSP licensing processes (quoting for new and renewal opportunities).

## Sub-stage 1.2 — Lead Outreach

Timely engagement with inbound and outbound leads. Sales teams follow structured outreach sequences, qualify prospects, and guide early conversations to determine whether an opportunity is worth pursuing.

Key actions:
- Within 24 hours of an outreach lead being assigned to Sales, add the lead to the appropriate lead sequence (based on topic of interest); follow the sequence until Qualified or Disqualified.
- Add prospecting contacts to Contact sequences and create a lead upon engagement; progress to Qualified or Disqualified following lead development activities.
- Leverage JourneyTeam tools, Microsoft GTM programs, and personal networks to generate, manage, and advance leads.
- Leverage reporting to help leadership coach on the correct areas of focus.

What "good" looks like: outreach is targeted and relevant · conversations focus on business outcomes · leads are engaged and qualified · next steps are clearly defined · handoffs are smooth and documented.

## Sub-stage 1.3 — Strategic Alignment

Key actions:
- Define Business Objectives and Outcomes.
- Identify issues that could impede or jeopardize objectives.
- Confirm BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline).
- Once BANT is confirmed, qualify the lead to create an opportunity.
- Leverage the Envision Sales Agent for coaching and suggested next steps.

What "good" looks like: business goals and outcomes clearly defined · flag plant is aligned · stakeholders engaged and aligned · scope and priorities understood · next steps clearly defined.

## Sub-stage 1.4 — Strategy Session

Key actions:
- Use information from Strategic Alignment to dive deeper into outcomes and issues.
- Collaborate closely with the customer to align the solution to their business outcomes — addressing the challenges preventing them from achieving objectives, ensuring the approach delivers measurable Value.
- Provide any showcases needed.
- Firm up the [Flag Plant](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/ProjectDelivery/SitePages/Project-Flag-Plant.aspx) (sets foundation for success).
- Leverage the Envision Sales Agent for coaching and suggested next steps.

What "good" looks like: goals and outcomes validated · flag plant clearly defined and aligned · approach understood and agreed · stakeholders aligned and confident · next steps toward proposal are clear.

## Sub-stage 1.5 — Solution Meeting

JourneyTeam presents the recommended solution and demonstrates how it will achieve the customer's desired outcomes. Confirms alignment on approach, value, and expectations before moving toward a formal engagement.

Key actions:
- Present how JourneyTeam will achieve desired outcomes with measurable results.
- Provide any showcase if needed.
- Agree on A-Grade Flag Plant and share pricing and ROI.
- Ensure both Prospect and JourneyTeam agree on: Business Outcomes · issues jeopardizing success · A-Grade Flag Plant · prospect acknowledgment of expectations met.
- Leverage the Envision Sales Agent for coaching and suggested next steps.

What "good" looks like: solution clearly presented and understood · approach aligns to goals and Flag Plant · scope and timeline clear · questions and risks addressed · stakeholders aligned and ready to move forward.

## Sub-stage 1.6 — EOW Sent

The Envision phase concludes with delivery of an Estimate of Work (EOW) — the agreed-upon solution, scope, and costs, providing the foundation for the customer to move forward.

Key items in an EOW: A-Grade Flag Plant included · details on how the solution will be delivered · details and costs.

What "good" looks like: EOW reflects agreed scope, timeline, and outcomes · pricing and deliverables clear · stakeholders aligned and ready to approve · questions minimal and addressed quickly · next steps to signature clear.

## Exit gate — Signed EOW required

Before moving from Envision to Plan:
1. Pre-approved by Practice Director (if applicable)
2. Includes [A-Grade Flag Plant](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/ProjectDelivery/SitePages/Project-Flag-Plant.aspx)
3. Signed copy saved in Quote folder on the account

# Stage 2: Plan — Building the Roadmap

The Plan stage prepares the project for execution by translating strategy into a clear, approved delivery plan. It begins after a signed EOW and continues until milestones, resources, and expectations are aligned and ready for delivery. Project Management and Delivery teams lead this phase.

**Who leads:** Holly Nesbit (Practice Director for PMO) · Biplab Mandal (COO). Full RASIC chart coming soon.

## How success is measured

**Project Milestones** *(metric being finalized)* — a milestone represents a significant event or outcome within a project, recognized by the customer and JourneyTeam, marking progress toward achieving the Flag Plant. Tracked in Project Operations at the task level.

Leading indicators:
- **Time from EOW signature to Target Start Date** — target: 4–6 weeks; Project Kick-Off should occur during the sprint matching the Target Start Date.
- **Initial Sprints planned** — target: 3 sprints consistently planned ([sprint planning](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/ProjectDelivery/SitePages/Project-Sprint-Planning.aspx); [Planned Work report](https://jtp.crm.dynamics.com/crmreports/viewer/viewer.aspx?action=run&id=6038d40a-630c-f111-8406-000d3a3529aa&helpID=Planned%20Work.rdl&appid=78854deb-627f-e911-a818-000d3a3b5b14)).
- **Functional Requirements** — target: artifacts completed within ~20% of overall project timeline.

## Sub-stage 2.1 — Project Kickoff

Brings JourneyTeam and the customer together to align on goals, confirm the flag plant, and establish the foundation for delivery.

Key actions:
- **Project Intake** — project repository set up in JTP with corresponding Teams/SharePoint.
- Project teams assembled by JT and customer.
- Internal Sales to Delivery Kickoff completed.
- Pre-kickoff alignment with customer PM + JT PM.
- Kickoff meetings for the internal project team and JT + customer teams (as needed).
- Flag Plant reviewed and confirmed with the customer; teams, governance, goals, roadmap, communication cadence confirmed.
- Support Strategy discussed.
- Training Strategy defined.
- Meeting cadence established and scheduled.

What "good" looks like: goals, scope, and success criteria aligned · flag plant confirmed · roles and responsibilities clear · communication cadence established · team aligned and ready to begin.

## Sub-stage 2.2 — Discovery

Brings JourneyTeam and the customer together to understand current and future state processes, define requirements, and identify key needs.

Key actions:
- Per each stakeholder group (change management).
- Current and future state process mapping.
- User story creation.

What "good" looks like: processes and requirements clearly defined · user needs and priorities validated · risks and gaps identified early · scope refined and aligned · teams aligned on what will be delivered.

## Sub-stage 2.3 — Document

Brings structure to the project by translating discovery insights into a clear, actionable plan.

Key actions:
- **Project plan complete in Planner** (timeline, milestones, deliverables, work breakdown structure):
  - Baseline budget (change orders executed if needed for increased scope per detailed discovery)
  - Exit gate deliverables for all Proven Process stages baselined (tracked in Project Operations as 'Deliverable' type)
  - Backlog created in Project Operations and milestones defined
  - Resource assignments established, accurately reflecting reality
  - Deliverable tasks built for the upcoming sprint, with a goal of three sprints planned
- **Governance and communication plan agreed** (decision rights, escalation path, status cadence, ceremonies):
  - Stakeholder registry
  - Adoption/Training approach drafted
  - Initial RAID log created with named owners and first mitigations
  - Change control approach identified before work starts
- **Architecture & Technical documentation approved** (per project needs) and linked to user stories.

What "good" looks like: project plan, milestones, deliverables defined · backlog created and prioritized · resources and assignments aligned · scope and budget baselined · planning artifacts complete and approved.

## Exit gate — Plan stage gate formally approved by PM, PL, and customer POC

Before moving to Deliver:
1. All planning artifacts, approvals, and baselines in place:
   - Business requirements defined and validated by the customer
   - Project plan baselined (change orders approved as necessary; deliverables for each exit gate defined and validated; assignments/resources defined; 3 sprints planned)
2. Architecture and Technical Documents completed (if applicable)
3. Flag Plant and Customer Impact confirmed

# Stage 3: Deliver — Bringing the Solution to Life

The Deliver stage brings the project to life through execution, where teams build, configure, and deliver solutions aligned to the flag plant. It begins once planning is complete and continues until the solution is validated and ready for launch.

**Who leads:** Ben Miller (Practice Director for CXC) · Biplab Mandal (COO). Full RASIC chart coming soon.

## How success is measured

**Customer Engagement Rate (CER)** — success measured by the level of active customer participation and engagement throughout delivery, ensuring alignment, adding value, timely feedback, and progress toward project outcomes. ([CER report](https://app.powerbi.com/groups/me/apps/b6a29afa-fcfa-4e68-9f30-767c1fb2a3b3/reports/9fd01160-de68-408a-b27c-255af72caa76/6f2698c0626b8ee464b4?ctid=b59c07c6-9496-463a-ab81-77697ac73d11&experience=power-bi))

**Customer Satisfaction (CSAT)** *(being finalized in Q2 2026)* — external measure of how customers perceive the value and quality delivered; whether execution, communication, and outcomes met or exceeded expectations.

Leading indicators by sub-stage:
- **Plan:** 3 sprints planned ahead (Green: 3 · Yellow: 2 · Red: 0–1).
- **Review:** On-budget (percent budget spent aligned with requirement progress: Percent Spent vs. Percent Complete = % features complete × budget-per-feature forecast) · Planned vs. Actual (Planned Work vs. CER Effort) · Progress of project (% features completed).
- **Execute:** [On-Time](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/ProjectDelivery/SitePages/Milestone-Planning.aspx) (% milestones on-time) · Sprint Commitment Met (% planned commitments completed).

## Sub-stage 3.1 — Plan (Internal Planning)

Brings JourneyTeam together to prepare for execution by defining sprint capacity, refining the backlog, and aligning on clear commitments.

Key actions:
- Define Sprint Capacity, groom backlog & lock in commitments.
- Commitments can range from designing, architecting, configuring & building systems, workflows, dashboards, integrations, migrations.
- Flag vague items; discuss and document changes.

What "great" looks like: 3 sprints planned and ready · backlog prioritized · commitments clearly defined · risks and dependencies documented · team aligned on next steps.

## Sub-stage 3.2 — Customer Review

Teams build, configure, and deliver against sprint commitments; progress actively managed to stay aligned to scope, timelines, and the flag plant.

Key actions:
- Before client review, ensure solution testing to success criteria.
- At end of sprint, review completion of committed deliverables with customers, including a functional demo / show & tell to ensure alignment with Flag Plant.
- Show and tell.
- Retro.

What "good" looks like: work clearly demonstrated and understood · deliverables meet expectations · feedback captured and addressed · risks and concerns proactively discussed · next steps and decisions clear.

## Sub-stage 3.3 — Execute

Bring the plan to life as teams build, configure, and deliver against sprint commitments.

Key actions:
- Monitor, track & manage commitments through sprint progress.

What "good" looks like: sprint commitments met · work on track with milestones · risks and blockers addressed quickly · deliverables meet success criteria · progress aligns to the flag plant.

## Exit gate — Production-Ready Approval

Before moving from Deliver to Launch:
1. Customer written sign-off on delivery that aligns with the project Flag Plant:
   1. **Production Readiness** — rollout, adoption, and support plans defined and validated
   2. **User Adoption Preparedness** — end-users enabled with training, communications, and success criteria
   3. **Support Readiness** — clear support model, ownership, and escalation paths in place
   4. **Post Go-Live Alignment** — backlog, known issues, and next steps documented and prioritized
   5. **Handoff Complete** — development and operational transition finalized and customer written sign-off obtained

# Stage 4: Launch — Going Live with Confidence

The Launch stage activates the solution by deploying to production, enabling users, and ensuring a smooth transition to operations. It begins once delivery is complete and continues through stabilization until the solution is fully adopted and ready for business as usual.

**Who leads:** Holly Nesbit (Practice Director for PMO) · Biplab Mandal (COO). Full RASIC chart coming soon.

## How success is measured

**Referenceable Customer** — success confirmed when the customer is satisfied with the outcome and willing to serve as a reference, reflecting strong delivery, adoption, and overall experience. ([Reference instructions](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/GrowthPerformance/SitePages/Customer-Reference-Instructions-2026.aspx?csf=1&web=1&e=jMajcY))

Leading indicators: being finalized — align to the intent above.

## Sub-stage 4.1 — Confirm

Ensures the solution, users, and environment are fully prepared for go-live.

Key actions:
- Code, configurations, and solutions finalized for the production environment (enforce code/config freeze).
- Production System Ready.
- End User Readiness.
- Escalation paths established and published.
- Support approach established.
- Rollback approach established.

What "good" looks like: solution and environment production-ready · users trained and prepared · deployment and rollback plans defined · support and escalation paths established · stakeholders aligned and ready for go-live.

## Sub-stage 4.2 — Deploy

Brings the solution into production by executing deployment and cutover activities.

Key actions:
- **Execute Deployment** — places the solution into the production-ready environment or state: technically available, not yet operational.
- **Execute Cutover** — activities necessary to operate using the new solution (data migration, integration processes, etc.), per the approved cutover plan.
- **Post-Cutover Validation** — a set period after cutover, before user access, to check production readiness (data accuracy, integrations, security). Typical duration: hours–days.
- **Go-Live** — the business milestone when users are enabled to begin using the system for real business work.

What "good" looks like: deployment and cutover run smoothly · systems, data, integrations function correctly · issues resolved quickly · users transition successfully · system live and operating as expected.

## Sub-stage 4.3 — Stabilize

Supports users and resolves issues following go-live.

Key actions:
- **Hypercare / Post Go-Live Support** — intensive, short-term support immediately following go-live; stabilize the environment, resolve issues quickly, support end users adapting to the new solution. Typical duration: 1–7 days, or as outlined in EOW.
- **Operational State** — following hypercare, formal operational transition; ownership transfers to operations and the project is closed. Duration depends on project requirements.

What "good" looks like: users supported during hypercare · issues resolved quickly · system stable and reliable · adoption progressing · transition to operations complete.

## Exit gate — Project Wrap

Before moving from Launch to Evolve:
1. **Customer Signoff**
   - Is the Flag Plant on track to be realized, or have outcomes been realized?
   - Is the project ready to transition to support?
2. **Operate**
   - The project team disengages from daily operations.
   - Transition to business as usual, with the client operation team taking responsibility for day-to-day activities.
3. **Project Retro & Sales Handoff**
   - Customer Retro: PM sends email to begin the retro process (projects ≥ 100 hours and Sherpa → Customer Advocacy; projects < 100 hours and not Sherpa → Sales, SAM or AE).
   - Sales Handoff: agreement to close project.
4. **Project Close**

# Stage 5: Evolve — Becoming a Trusted Advisor

The Evolve stage focuses on maximizing long-term value by strengthening the customer relationship, driving adoption, and identifying new opportunities. It begins after launch and continues through ongoing support, optimization, and strategic engagement. Customer Advocacy, Support, and Account teams lead this phase.

**Who leads:** Mark Mickelsen (VP of Client Solutions) · Tim Brandt (SEC Practice Manager) · Drew Peterson (CRO) · Biplab Mandal (COO). Full RASIC chart coming soon.

## How success is measured

**Customer Retention** — measured on billed non-CSP in the last 12 months. Report updated monthly.

**New Non-Support Projects** *(metric being finalized)* — percentage of customers with a second non-support project created within a running 12-month period.

Leading indicators:
1. Customer retention at 6 months *(currently only 12-month reporting available)*.
2. Opportunities Created with Existing Customer.
3. Support Case Success/Resolution.
4. Frequency of Customer Communication *(being finalized)*: 70 QBRs performed per quarter (as a team) · sequence outreach · all-customer quarterly email & webinar.

## Sub-stage 5.1 — Validate

Brings JourneyTeam and the customer together to confirm outcomes, measure impact, and ensure alignment with the flag plant.

Key items:
- Validate Impact & Flag Plant.

What "good" looks like: outcomes and impact are measured · value aligns to the flag plant · adoption and usage validated · feedback gathered and reviewed · next opportunities identified.

## Sub-stage 5.2 — Manage

Ongoing engagement ensuring strong relationships, proactive communication, and alignment on evolving business needs.

**Customer Advocacy:**
- Completion of Project Retrospective — preferred: live customer retrospective discussion. If a discussion can't be secured: an internal assessment (insights from Project Team, Sales, Finance) reviewed by the Internal Validation Committee for whether the project is a positive or negative add to the Customer Referencability calculation.
- Live retrospective outputs: whether the customer joins the Testimonial or Case Study pipeline (managed by Customer Advocacy) and/or the Customer Reference Program.

**Trusted Advisor:**
- **Step 1 — Set up the expectation:** establish what a trusted advisory relationship is vs. a vendor relationship. What we will do: conduct Quarterly Business Reviews · provide valuable information & insights relevant to the customer and their industry · communicate weekly with relevant updates that help the customer move their business forward. What the customer needs to do: agree to Strategic/Quarterly Business Reviews · agree to weekly communication · provide business KPIs and details on strategic initiatives.
- **Step 2 — Agreement to be strategic.**
- **Weekly Connections.**
- **SBR (Strategic Business Review):** first 15 minutes — validate adoption and usage of current systems, identify and address gaps, identify new project phases or beneficial functionality; 45+ minutes — discuss current business challenges and strategic initiatives. ([SBR process and scripts](https://journeyteam.sharepoint.com/sites/sales/_layouts/15/guestaccess.aspx?share=IgD0TPlfCs6KQ5hy73q0aSqFARpjzFPb7SxeF2U_silZ08E&e=Rfudn7))

What "good" looks like: regular communication and engagement maintained · trusted advisor relationship established and strengthened · customer needs, priorities, and challenges continuously understood · strategic conversations (QBRs) conducted consistently · risks, concerns, opportunities proactively addressed · alignment maintained on current and future direction.

## Sub-stage 5.3 — Optimize

Enhancing the solution, supporting user adoption, and identifying opportunities to improve performance and drive additional value.

Key items:
- **Support Onboarding and Education** — owner pending; within one week of Support Contract start; SOP coming soon.
- **SLA Achievement — Triage** (owner: practice-designated triage lead). Guaranteed response times per support agreement: Bronze — no SLA · Silver — 6-hour · Gold — 4-hour · Platinum — 2-hour. A "response" includes: acknowledgment the case is received and reviewed; severity clarification (low, normal, high, critical, or enhancement — based on business priority & end-user impact); an update on next steps.
  - Case submissions estimated above 20 hours of effort are leads, not support cases — forwarded to account managers.
  - Customer response SLAs tracked to contractual agreements; internal resolution SLAs to be defined and tracked; tier 2/3 escalation parameters TBD.
- **Case to Close Turnaround** — time between case submission and closure as resolved (owner: case owner; severity-based expectations to be determined; doc coming soon).
- **Knowledge Base Documentation Updated** — an expectation once the capability is available in Project Operations; part of case-closure activities (owner: case owner).
- **Support Contracts Active and Current** — within a 12-month period.

What "good" looks like: solution continuously improved · adoption strong and growing · support effective and timely · performance optimized · new growth opportunities identified.
